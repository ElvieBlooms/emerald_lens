1 Number the sprites

Start with the sheet you want to use: a fan game sheet, a collage, or a folder of one image per action (a .zip works too). The tools cut out every sprite and number it, so a recipe can say which sprite goes where.

Already drew on our own template? Skip to packing.

Drop a sheet, several images, or a .zip here

Slicing options

Scale: how many times the sheet is enlarged. Backdrop: the flat colour around the sprites, like #ff7f27. Reach: how far apart pixels can be and still count as one sprite. Lossy cleanup and colours only matter for JPEG or WebP sheets.

3 Pack into the game's sheets

  
Packing cuts a filled sheet into the files the mod reads. Use the sheets from step 2, or your own: a combined sheet, an extras sheet, or a folder of separate templates. Blank templates: FR/LG, R/S/E, FR/LG wide, R/S/E wide, FR/LG extras, R/S/E extras.

    Combine sheets, or add new art to a finished sheet
    
Put partial sheets together frame by frame: walking from one, biking from another. Each frame comes from whichever source drew it. Where two sources drew the same frame differently, you choose which to keep. To add to a finished sheet, put the finished one first.

    4 Name the look and download it
    
  
    A look is one folder with a meta.json and the packed art. Pack both game families into it if you have art for both. Unzip the download into the mod's assets/sprites folder and restart the game.
    
  
    
    
  
    
  
    
    
    
    
    
    
    
  
    
  
    The options menu shows 8 characters of a label or character name. Looks of the same character are grouped under it as LOOK choices. Generation sets the fallback order: a sheet this look doesn't have comes from the character's looks of the same generation first.
